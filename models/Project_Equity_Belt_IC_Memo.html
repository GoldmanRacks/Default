ISituation

Labor dislocation

Nearly 1,400 workers were laid off at the Navistar truck plant in Springfield, OH, enough to erase a year of job growth in greater Dayton. The plant transferred to Roshel on Oct 2, and rehiring is uncertain.(1) That leaves a trained pool of assembly workers, technicians and drivers available within 25 miles.

Carrier distress

At least 16 U.S. carriers filed for bankruptcy from late August through September as diesel reached a record $6.37/gal. Eight were midsize-or-larger Chapter 11s; the rest were mostly 1–6 truck Chapter 7 liquidations.(2) Ohio filings in 2026 include Platinum Express (Chapter 11, May) and TJ Trucking (Chapter 11, July).(9)

Demand anchors

Arsenal-1 (Anduril, Pickaway County) is producing from Building 1. Building 2 (more than 924k sq ft) is under construction, and the site has a phased buildout to 2035 with 4,008 jobs.(3) The Central Ohio data center corridor needs oversize freight: transformers, switchgear and modular electrical rooms. QTS is co-owned by BIP and BREIT.(4)

IIInvestment thesis

  • Buy below replacement cost at a late-cycle low. A Dalio-style late-cycle energy shock is forcing leveraged small operators to sell. Bankruptcy routes acquire carriers at 2.5–3.5x normalized EBITDA and fleets at 50–65% of replacement cost.
  • Specialized, contracted freight, not commodity dry van. Dedicated contracts with Arsenal-1 contractors and QTS carry fuel-surcharge pass-through and multi-year terms, which reduces exposure to the spot-rate cycle.
  • Employee ownership as the operating lever. Under the Ownership Works model, all ~1,500 employees receive equity. At KKR's C.H.I. Overhead Doors, the same model accompanied a 4x EBITDA increase and an average $175k payout to hourly workers.(5)(6) The main value driver in trucking is retention: we underwrite driver turnover falling from 60% to 20%.
  • Cross-platform synergy within Blackstone. BIP's QTS ownership provides a captive anchor customer. BIP's long-duration capital is suited to the hard assets and to an eventual sale to an employee stock ownership plan (ESOP).
  • Policy tailwinds. The bipartisan Employee Equity Investment Act (Rubio / Moore / Van Hollen) and the Empowering Small-Business Ownership Act would lower the cost of ESOP conversion and open Defense Department contracting to ESOPs. Neither is in the base case.(5)(7)

IIITransaction structure: why Tac Opps and BIP together

Tac Opps capacity is constrained, so the deal is split along asset lines. BIP takes the hard, contracted, long-lived assets. Tac Opps takes the operating turnaround and the equity upside. This cuts the Tac Opps check from $111M (single-fund structure) to $61M.

IXPolitical necessity: why before November 3

The Springfield layoffs were announced days before a presidential rally in the area. That put a blue-collar job loss at the center of the last month of the midterm campaign.(1)(10) The internal research paper behind this deal argues that broad-based employee ownership is the policy answer both parties are now competing to own.(5) For Blackstone, announcing before November 3 turns a cyclical distressed trade into the reference case for that debate.

1 · The window closes on Nov 3

Public attention on the layoffs and the bankruptcy wave peaks before Election Day. After it, the story moves on and the 120th Congress sets its agenda in January 2027. A live Ohio platform with employee-owners on payroll gives that agenda a ready-made proof point.

2 · Both parties want the same voter

Non-college, working-class voters decide Rust Belt races. The paper describes a "policy vacuum": loyalty that is cultural but not yet economic. Employee ownership is one of the few positions with cross-party support: 72% of Republicans and 74% of Democrats prefer to work for an employee-owned company.(5)

3 · Reversing the "vulture capital" story

Private equity is usually cast as the cause of plant closures and layoffs. This deal reverses that: Blackstone capital arrives after the layoffs and makes drivers and technicians owners. Following the C.H.I. template, it becomes "PE that turns workers into owners," which reduces political and regulatory pressure on the asset class.(5)(6)

4 · Dalio's internal-conflict risk

A record wealth gap plus a downturn (the diesel shock and freight recession) is Dalio's setup for rising populism. Capital that visibly shares the gains with workers is a hedge for the franchise: it shows market mechanisms closing the gap without redistribution.(5)

5 · Legislative leverage

The EEIA (Rubio / Moore / Van Hollen) and the Empowering Small-Business Ownership Act (Mills) are pending. A working Ohio case before the new Congress improves their odds. Passage would cut the cost of the ESOP exit (Exit B) and of buying from retiring owners (route E), and open Defense Department contracting.(5)(7)

6 · Workforce timing

Roshel's ramp-up at the former Navistar plant will rehire some of the laid-off workforce. Hiring and training offers made in October capture the best technicians and drivers first. After the election, that pool shrinks.(1)
