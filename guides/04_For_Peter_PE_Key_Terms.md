## 1. Players and Fund Structure

The private equity ecosystem is organized around a small number of entities whose relationships are defined in the Limited Partnership Agreement. The GP controls investment decisions and earns carry; LPs supply the overwhelming majority of capital under limited liability.

| Term | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| GP (General Partner) | Entity that controls the fund and makes investment decisions; unlimited liability (usually held via an LLC) | Earns carry; legally responsible |
| LP (Limited Partner) | Investor (pensions, endowments, sovereigns, insurers, family offices, HNW); liability limited to commitment | Supplies roughly 95–99% of capital |
| Management Company (ManCo) | Adviser entity that employs the team and receives management fees | Registered investment adviser; holds the economics of running the firm |
| Fund (Limited Partnership) | Usually a Delaware or Cayman LP; closed-end, ~10 years plus extensions | The legal pool of capital |
| LPA | Governing contract: economics, governance, term, key person provisions | Everything is negotiated here |
| PPM | Offering document describing strategy, team, risks and terms | Disclosure document |
| Side letter | Bilateral LP-specific terms (fee breaks, reporting, co-invest, ESG or sanctions exclusions) | Usually accompanied by an MFN clause |
| MFN (Most Favored Nation) | LP can elect any better term given to a similar-size LP | Limits preferential deals |
| LPAC | Committee of large LPs that approves conflicts, valuations and extensions | Governance check on the GP |
| Feeder / Parallel / AIV / Blocker | Vehicles used for tax, regulatory or investor-access reasons; same underlying deals | Routes particular investments or LP types efficiently |
| SPV / Co-investment | Single-asset vehicle, often for co-investment at low or zero fee and carry | Lowers blended fees; lets the GP write bigger checks |
| Continuation vehicle (CV) | GP-led secondary: assets move from an old fund to a new vehicle; existing LPs roll or sell | Extends the hold on winners |
| Holdco / Sleeve | Holding company owning stakes across sub-portfolios or distinct sub-strategies. <span class="xref">▸</span> Leviathan core PE holdco: $1,406M across three sleeves (IQT-mirror $250M, Weapons $956M, Consumer / Value $200M) | Multi-strategy exposure within one vehicle |
| GP commitment | GP’s own capital in the fund (typically 1–5%) | Alignment (“skin in the game”) |
| Key person / No-fault divorce | Investment period suspends if named individuals leave; LPs can remove the GP by supermajority without cause | Protects LPs against team risk; the ultimate LP lever |

## 2. Fund Lifecycle

A typical closed-end PE fund runs 10 + 2 years. Capital is called during the investment period (roughly years 1–5), value is created through years 3–8, and harvest and distributions dominate years 4–12.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Commitment | Total capital an LP agrees to provide over the fund’s life |
| First / final close | First and last dates LPs can join; later closers pay equalization interest |
| Capital call (drawdown) | GP requests a portion of commitments, usually with ~10 business days’ notice |
| Paid-in capital (PIC) | Cumulative capital actually called |
| Unfunded commitment / Dry powder | Commitment not yet called; industry-wide or fund-level uncalled capital available to invest |
| Investment period | Window (~5 years) in which new platforms can be acquired |
| Harvest period | Post-investment period: follow-ons and exits only |
| Recycling | Reinvesting early proceeds (up to a cap) instead of distributing them |
| Subscription line | Bank loan secured by LP commitments; delays calls and inflates IRR |
| NAV loan | Fund-level borrowing secured by portfolio NAV; used for liquidity or follow-ons |
| Vintage year | Year of first investment or first close; used for peer comparison |
| Follow-on reserve | Capital set aside for existing portfolio companies. <span class="xref">▸</span> IQT sleeve: $25M of $250M |

## 3. Fund Economics

### 3.1 Fees

| Term | Definition | Typical |
|---|---|---|
| Management fee | Annual fee to the ManCo | 1.5–2.0% of committed capital during the investment period, stepping down thereafter. <span class="xref">▸</span> Model uses 2% of committed for the full life |
| Fee offset | Share of transaction and monitoring fees credited against the management fee | 80–100% offset today |
| Transaction / monitoring fees | Fees the GP charges portfolio companies | Increasingly offset or banned |
| Organizational expenses | Fund formation costs charged to the fund (capped) | e.g. $1–3M cap |
| Fund expenses | Audit, admin, legal, broken-deal costs | Charged to the fund |

### 3.2 Carry and hurdle

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Carried interest (carry) | GP’s share of profits, typically 20% (range 10–30%) |
| Preferred return (hurdle) | Minimum LP return before carry is paid, usually 8% compounded annually |
| Catch-up | After the pref, the GP takes a large share (often 100%) until it has caught up to its 20% of total profits |
| European (whole-fund) waterfall | Carry only after LPs have received all contributed capital plus pref across the entire fund. LP-friendly |
| American (deal-by-deal) waterfall | Carry paid on each realized deal (usually after returning that deal’s cost plus allocated expenses). GP-friendly; needs a clawback |
| Clawback | GP must return carry already received if the final fund result does not support it |
| Hard vs soft hurdle | Hard: carry only on profits above the hurdle (no catch-up). Soft: once the hurdle is met, carry applies to all profit (with catch-up) |

### 3.3 Worked waterfall: $100M fund, $250M gross proceeds, 5-year single exit

Pref = 100 × (1.08<sup>5</sup> − 1) = **$46.93M**. Total profit = **$150M**.

| Tier | Full catch-up (soft hurdle) | No catch-up (hard hurdle) <span class="xref">▸</span> Leviathan |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Return of capital (LP) | 100.00 | 100.00 |
| 2. Preferred 8% (LP) | 46.93 | 46.93 |
| 3. GP catch-up (100%) | 11.73 (= 46.93 × 20 / 80) | — |
| 4. Remaining split 80 / 20 | LP 73.07 / GP 18.27 | LP 82.46 / GP 20.61 |
| GP carry total | 30.00 (= 20% of 150) | 20.61 |
| LP total (before fees) | 220.00 | 229.39 |

Fees are then deducted: 2% × $100M × 5 years = $10M. The difference between the two waterfalls (~$9.4M here) grows with performance.

## 4. Performance Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MOIC | Total value ÷ invested capital | Ignores time. Gross (deal level) vs net (to LP) |
| IRR | Discount rate r such that Σ CF<sub>t</sub> / (1 + r)<sup>t</sup> = 0 | Time-weighted; sensitive to timing and subscription lines. Single-exit shortcut: MOIC<sup>1/T</sup> − 1 |
| TVPI | (Cumulative distributions + NAV) ÷ PIC | Net multiple, realized plus unrealized |
| DPI | Cumulative distributions ÷ PIC | “Cash-on-cash”: what LPs care about most in tight markets |
| RVPI | NAV ÷ PIC | Unrealized portion; TVPI = DPI + RVPI |
| PME / KS-PME | Compares PE cash flows with an index. KS-PME = [Σ Dist<sub>t</sub> × (I<sub>T</sub> / I<sub>t</sub>) + NAV<sub>T</sub>] ÷ Σ Call<sub>t</sub> × (I<sub>T</sub> / I<sub>t</sub>) | >1.0 = beat the index. Single in / out: MOIC ÷ (1 + r<sub>index</sub>)<sup>T</sup>. <span class="xref">▸</span> 5-year Base: 2.42x ÷ 1.09<sup>5</sup> = 1.57x vs the S&P 500 |
| Direct Alpha | IRR of index-compounded cash flows | Annualized excess return over the index |
| J-curve | Early negative returns (fees, write-downs) before value creation and exits turn the curve up | Subscription lines flatten it artificially |
| Gross-to-net spread | Gross IRR − net IRR | Typically 4–7 percentage points for buyout |
| Loss ratio | % of capital invested in deals returning < 1.0x | Measures underwriting discipline |

**Common traps:** IRR can be gamed with subscription lines and quick flips. MOIC ignores time. Interim NAVs are Level 3 marks (judgment). Always look at DPI and PME together.

## 5. Strategies

| Strategy | Profile | Control / leverage | Return driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venture capital | Pre-profit, high growth, power-law outcomes | Minority, no leverage | A few outsized winners |
| Growth equity | Revenue scaling, near or at profitability | Minority, little leverage | Revenue growth |
| Buyout / LBO | Mature, cash-generative | Control, 4–7x debt / EBITDA | EBITDA growth, deleveraging, multiple |
| Take-private (P2P) | Buying a listed company | Control | Re-rating plus operational improvement |
| Carve-out / spin-off | Buying a division from a corporate parent | Control or minority | Standalone efficiency; TSAs |
| Buy-and-build / roll-up | Platform plus add-on acquisitions | Control | Multiple arbitrage (buy add-ons at 6x, consolidate at 12x) |
| Secondaries (LP-led) | Buying LP fund interests | Passive | Discount to NAV, shorter J-curve |
| Secondaries (GP-led) | Continuation vehicles, strip sales | Varies | Extending winners |
| Private credit / direct lending | Senior loans to sponsor-backed companies | Lender | Floating-rate spread |
| Distressed / special situations | Stressed balance sheets | Loan-to-own | Restructuring |

## 7. Valuation

<div class="formula">Equity Value = Enterprise Value − Debt − Preferred − Minority interest − Debt-like items + Cash</div>

Debt-like items include leases, unfunded pensions and, in some deals, deferred revenue.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| EBITDA / Adjusted EBITDA | Operating cash proxy. “Adjusted” removes one-offs and is where sellers inflate the number |
| EV / EBITDA, EV / EBIT, EV / Revenue | Core multiples. <span class="xref">▸</span> LHX 15.1x and RHM 17.3x EV / EBITDA; Saronic $9.25B on $200M revenue (~46x EV / revenue) |
| Trading comps / Precedent transactions | Multiples of similar listed companies / multiples paid in comparable M&A (include a control premium) |
| DCF / LBO analysis | PV of free cash flow plus terminal value / maximum price a sponsor can pay and still hit its target IRR |
| Pre-money / post-money | Value before / after new money. Post = Pre + new investment. Ownership = Check ÷ Post. <span class="xref">▸</span> Hermeus: $35M ÷ $1.0B Series C post = 3.5% |
| Option pool shuffle | Pool created in the pre-money, so the dilution falls on existing holders |
| ASC 820 / Level 3 | Fair-value accounting; private marks are model-based (Level 3) |
| Probability-weighted value | Σ p<sub>i</sub> × Value<sub>i</sub> across scenarios. <span class="xref">▸</span> Holdco 7-year Expected = 45% × 3.56x + 25% × 7.79x + 15% × 1.05x + 15% × 6.14x = 4.63x net MOIC |

## 8. Capital Structure and LBO Mechanics

**Stack, senior → junior:** revolver → Term Loan A → Term Loan B / unitranche → second lien → senior / sub notes → mezzanine / PIK → HoldCo PIK → preferred equity → common equity (sponsor + management).

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revolver (RCF) / TLA / TLB | Drawable working-capital line / amortizing bank loan / institutional, mostly bullet, floating rate |
| Unitranche / Second lien | Single blended senior loan / junior secured claim on the same collateral |
| Mezzanine / PIK | Subordinated debt with warrants or equity kicker / interest that accrues to principal |
| Leverage / Interest coverage / FCCR | Debt ÷ EBITDA / EBITDA ÷ interest / (EBITDA − capex − tax) ÷ fixed charges |
| Maintenance vs incurrence covenants | Tested quarterly vs only when taking an action |
| Cov-lite | No maintenance covenants (common in TLB) |
| Sources & uses | Table showing where the money comes from and where it goes |
| Dividend recap / Deleveraging | Borrowing to pay the sponsor a dividend (DPI without an exit) / using FCF to repay debt |
| MIP / sweet equity | Management equity, often 5–15% of common, with ratchets |

### 8.1 Worked LBO

Entry EBITDA $50M × 10x = EV $500M, funded with 5x debt ($250M) and $250M equity. In year 5, EBITDA $80M × 10x = EV $800M; net debt is paid down to $120M, so equity = **$680M**.

## 9. Equity Terms (Venture, Growth, Minority)

The terms that protect a minority investor matter most for stakes like the Leviathan ones.

| Term | Definition | Investor-friendly version |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidation preference | Paid before common in an exit | 1x non-participating is standard; >1x or participating is aggressive |
| Participating preferred | Gets its preference back, then shares pro rata | “Double dip”; often capped (e.g. 3x) |
| Anti-dilution | Price protection in down rounds | Full ratchet (strong) vs broad-based weighted average (standard) |
| Pro rata / preemptive right | Right to keep ownership % in future rounds | Key for follow-on reserves |
| ROFR | Right of first refusal on share transfers | Controls the cap table |
| Tag-along / Drag-along | Minority can sell alongside the majority / majority can force the minority to sell | Essential minority protection / minority wants a price floor |
| Protective provisions | Consent needed for new senior securities, sale, debt, budget | Key minority control |
| Board seat / observer | Governance or visibility | <span class="xref">▸</span> Sought at >15% ownership |
| Information rights | Monthly or quarterly financials, budget, audit | Basic |
| Redemption right | Investor can force a buyback after N years | Liquidity path in family- or ESOP-owned companies |
| Warrants | Right to buy shares at a strike | Equity kicker; common in mezzanine, SPAC and structured deals |
| Convertible note / SAFE | Early instruments converting at the next round, with cap and discount | Seed stage |

**Dilution and retention:** if a fund owns 10% today and new rounds sell 15%, 10% and 10%, final ownership = 10% × 0.85 × 0.90 × 0.90 = 6.9% (retention 0.69). <span class="xref">▸</span> The model’s 0.68 retention at 10 years for the IQT sleeve encodes the same idea.

|---|---|

## 13. Founder / GP Economics: Worked Example for One Vehicle

This section works through GP economics for **one of the four Leviathan vehicles**: the core PE holdco, as modeled in the January 2027 internal model (**$1,406M** committed: IQT-mirror $250M, Weapons $956M, Consumer / Value $200M). The other three vehicles also contribute to GP and founder compensation; they are excluded here and their contribution is not stated.

| Assumption | Basis |
|---|---|
| Fund terms | 2% management fee on committed capital for the full hold; 20% carry; 8% compounded preferred return |
| Waterfall | Whole-fund European, hard hurdle, no GP catch-up: carry only on profit above return of capital plus pref |
| GP ownership | Blackstone as a 50% silent GP underwriter (no operational role); the remaining 50% split equally between the two operating founders, Donald B Ritts III and Peter McCarthy |
| Case used | **Blend**: simple average of the model’s Base, Bull and probability-weighted Expected cases |

### 13.3 More capital, not more GP stakes

**More money can be raised.** This example is sized at the modeled $1,406M; it is not a cap. Additional LP commitments, into this vehicle or the other three, scale both fee and carry dollars, and because LP capital carries no GP ownership, every dollar raised accrues to the existing GP split. At the same returns, each additional **$100M** of commitments adds roughly **$108M** to the 7-year blend GP pool, about **$27M per founder**.

**No further GP stakes are being considered.** Selling GP equity is a permanent claim on every future fee and carry dollar across all four vehicles and any successor funds. Blackstone’s 50% silent stake already provides GP capital and sponsorship; growth should come from LP capital, which does not dilute ownership.

| Additional GP stake sold (pro rata) | Each founder’s share | 7-year blend, per founder | Cost per founder |
|---|---|---|---|
| None (current structure) | 25.0% | $381M | — |
| 10% | 22.5% | $343M | −$38M |
| 20% | 20.0% | $305M | −$76M |
